The problem

The IT department tracked its tasks and projects in manual Excel files. The IT Manager needed one place to see progress, spot delays and check who was available.

What I did

  • Led the project as lead developer and QA in a 2-person team. I planned it, created and delegated the tasks between me and my co-developer, and followed it through the Agile cycle from requirements to deployment.
  • Met the IT Manager and IT staff to gather requirements, raised clarifying questions and suggested improvements so the scope was clear before building.
  • Designed the database from scratch: the projects and tasks tables, planned versus actual dates, and soft deletion that keeps history. Task status is calculated on the fly instead of stored.
  • Developed the My Tasks page (add, sort, filter, edit, delete), the Projects management page with full CRUD, search and admin-only access, the Analytics page, the unassigned tasks pool, and role-based access with session control. I designed the UI and UX of these, responsive on mobile, tablet and desktop.
  • Built the workflow rules and transaction-safe checks that stop a developer from running two tasks at once, then optimized the SQL and cut the polling load so live updates stay fast.
  • Tested my own code, features and UI/UX, then QA'd my co-developer's modules too, so both parts worked well together and matched what we agreed.
  • Made the project presentation deck, presented the system to the IT Manager and IT staff, and deployed and tested updates on the live Arktech server.

What I built

My part of a project and task tracker for the IT department: the database, the My Tasks and Projects pages (with admin-only controls and soft deletion), and an Analytics page with Work State and Schedule Status charts, plus an Action Required list for overdue and due-today tasks that my co-developer and I built together, where I refined the logic and shaped the UI design. Updates appear live without a page reload. Each task's status (Advanced, On Track or Delayed) is calculated automatically from its target dates, and developers can't run two tasks at once. Managers control every task and developers manage their own.

Testing and feedback

Functional, integration and user acceptance testing, with rounds of stakeholder feedback. My features were tested by me and cross-checked by my co-developer. After we presented the system, the IT Manager approved the recommendations for the next phase of development.

The problem

Task management for 10 departments needed to move into one centralized system, where managers could see what each person was working on and catch delays early.

What I did

  • Led the project as lead developer and QA in a 2-person team. I planned it, created and delegated the tasks with my co-developer, and met the IT Manager and department staff to gather requirements before building.
  • Designed the database tables for tasks, attachments and action logs.
  • Developed the My Tasks page, the department pool for unassigned tasks, the Analytics page with CSV import, the file and evidence attachments (drag-and-drop, image previews, size limits) and the action logs that keep old and new values (my co-developer later polished and populated them), and role-based access for staff, department admins and super admins. I designed the UI and UX of these.
  • Expanded the dashboard, analytics and personal task modules for multi-department use and, together with my co-developer, reorganized MyTasks and Analytics into cleaner modules and added lazy loading so the pages stay fast as records grew.
  • Built an impersonation feature for role-based testing, tested my own code and features, then QA'd my co-developer's modules so they worked well and stayed aligned with mine.
  • Made the project deck and demonstrated the system live, with my co-developer, to the IT Manager and stakeholders across 10 departments over 3 sessions, then presented to the Company President together, and handled their feedback.

What I built

My part of a company-wide task system for 10 departments: the database, the My Tasks page, a department pool where unassigned tasks wait until someone picks them up, and an Analytics page with Work State and Schedule Status charts and bulk CSV import. Files and evidence can be attached to any task, every change is saved in an action log with its old and new values, and a master list controls access for super admins (all departments), department admins (their own) and staff (My Tasks).

Functional, integration and user acceptance testing, with rounds of stakeholder feedback. Each live session gathered feedback from the departments, and the project ended with a final presentation to the Company President.

The problem

Daily machine maintenance checks were recorded on paper. Sheets could be lost or damaged, records were hard to back up, and nothing stopped a checker from skipping steps.

What I did

  • Led the project from the ground up as project lead, lead developer and QA. I planned the system, created and delegated nearly all of the tasks, 125 in total tracked in our IT Project Visualization system, 81 mine and 44 for my co-developer, 42 of which I assigned.
  • Gathered requirements from the IT Manager, my supervisor and the target users, pitched the idea to executives, and ran the consultation rounds whose confirmed bug fixes and change requests went into the final version.
  • Designed the database tables (templates, check entries, check logs and machine QR tokens) and created and linked them in the Arktech database.
  • Developed the Checker and Verifier modules (QR scanning, step forms with progress, NG photo capture, review and submit, verdict review, revision and re-evaluation rounds), the login and access control, and the connection to the Arktech V4 portal. I designed their UI and UX and wrote the PDF export code.
  • Tested my own modules as I built them, on real company phones and under stress, audited access for every role, and QA'd my co-developer's Admin module so everything stayed aligned and worked well.
  • Made the project deck and demo, presented the system to stakeholders from 2 departments alongside the IT Manager, and ran a user session for department revision requests.
  • After my co-developer's last tasks on May 13, I finished the rest on my own: Admin polish and PDF styling, the restricted and no-access pages, code cleanup, final smoke and stress tests, and the final presentation and demo to company executives on May 18. I also wrote the user manual, workflow guide, developer guide, READMEs and schema documentation for turnover.

What I built

A digital checksheet for 4 machines with three roles: Checkers and Verifiers on mobile, Admins on desktop. I built the Checker and Verifier modules end to end. Checkers scan the machine's QR, then each checkpoint's QR, before answering OK or NG (or a numeric value against set limits), with a photo required for every NG. Verifiers approve or reject each checkpoint, rejected ones return for re-inspection while approved ones stay locked. My co-developer built the Admin module (machines and QR codes, templates, dashboard, logs), which I tested, polished and took over to finish the remaining tasks. Admins export PDFs that match the paper checksheets, and access is limited by department and position.

Testing and feedback

Unit, integration, smoke and stress testing on the live server, real-device QR scanning tests and user acceptance testing, with rounds of stakeholder feedback. Change requests from target users went into the final version, and the presentation gathered feedback on the move from paper to digital.
